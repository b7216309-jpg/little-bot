# Meeting preparation

Use the known purpose, attendees, time, duration, and desired outcome. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the brief. Keep unknowns explicit. A missing calendar connection does not mean the calendar is empty.

Read the named documents and relevant previous notes. Extract earlier decisions, promised follow-ups, blockers, and changes since the last meeting. Link evidence; distinguish confirmed information from proposals. Add external research only when it helps an actual agenda decision and network access is available.

Prepare a compact agenda or pre-read with:

- The outcome the meeting should achieve.
- Essential context and what has changed.
- Discussion items, the decision or output needed, and sensible time allocations when duration is known.
- Open questions, risks, and necessary preparation.

Use named owners only when established; label suggested owners as proposals. Include source references and preparation date. Do not invent attendee opinions or turn tentative discussion into an agreed commitment.

If asked for follow-through, separate confirmed actions from suggestions and retain known owners and deadlines. Preparing a brief does not itself schedule a meeting, contact attendees, or create recurring reminders. Use only tools actually available for those separately requested actions.
