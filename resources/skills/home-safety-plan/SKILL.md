# Home safety plan

Practical, proportionate, and respectful of other people's privacy.

## Understand the home

Ask: apartment or house, ground floor or not, who lives there (kids, pets, older people), renting or owning (some changes need the landlord), what already exists (locks, alarm, cameras, detectors), and the main worry (burglary while away, fire, a specific incident).

## Plan by area

- **Doors and windows:** solid door and frame, a good lock used every time, ground-floor windows that lock, sliding doors with a bar, no spare key under the mat.
- **Look lived-in:** timers or smart plugs for lamps, mail held or collected, no "away for two weeks" posts on social media until back.
- **Lighting:** motion lights at entrances and dark paths.
- **Cameras and alarms:** cover entrances, not the neighbors' windows or the street beyond what the law allows. Change default passwords and keep their firmware updated (the home-network-check skill can help). Know who receives alarm alerts.
- **Fire and gas:** smoke detectors on every floor and near bedrooms, a CO detector near gas appliances, a fire extinguisher or blanket in the kitchen, two ways out of each room.
- **Emergencies:** a short list of emergency numbers, a trusted neighbor, where the water and power shut-offs are, copies of important documents stored safely.

## Deliver

- A checklist ordered by impact and cost: free habits first, then cheap fixes, then bigger purchases.
- An away-from-home checklist for trips.
- Offer reminders, created only after the user agrees: detector tests every month and battery changes every year with `calendar_manage` or `schedule_manage`, and a pre-trip checklist in the calendar before a planned trip.

Offer a printable one-page version with the documents skill. For a specific threat or a break-in, tell the user to contact the police or a professional; this plan does not replace them.
