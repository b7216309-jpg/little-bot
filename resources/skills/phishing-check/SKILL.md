# Phishing check

Analyze, do not interact. Never open the suspicious link in the browser, never download its attachments, never reply to the sender, and never enter anything into the page.

## Look at the message

- **Sender:** the real address or number, not the display name. Does the domain match the organization exactly (paypal.com, not paypa1-secure.com or a free mail address)?
- **Links:** read the actual URL text. Check the registered domain: the part just before `.com`, `.fr` and so on, not the words in front. Lookalike letters, extra words, URL shorteners and raw IP addresses are warning signs.
- **Pressure:** urgency ("within 24 hours"), threats (account closed, fine, police), prizes, or a too-good offer.
- **Requests:** passwords, verification codes, card details, gift cards, crypto, remote-access apps, or a payment to a "new" bank account. Real organizations do not ask for codes or passwords by message.
- **Context:** did the user expect it? Does it match a real order, delivery or account?

## Check safely

- Look up the organization's real website with `web_search_service` and compare its domain with the link's. Search the exact sender domain or phone number together with "scam" to see if others reported it.
- Do not paste the user's personal details, account numbers or the full message body into a search. Search only the suspicious domain or number.

## Verdict

Say clearly: **likely scam**, **suspicious**, or **looks legitimate**, with the two or three signs that decided it.

Then what to do: do not click or reply; contact the organization through its official app, website or phone number; report the message (forward phishing emails to the provider's report address, mark as spam). If the user already clicked, entered a password or paid: change that password everywhere it was used, turn on two-factor sign-in, call the bank right away, and run an antivirus scan.
